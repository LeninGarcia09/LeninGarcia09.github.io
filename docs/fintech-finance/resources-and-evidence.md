## Portfolio specifications

Keep the two projects small, reproducible, and clearly labeled as **independent learning projects using synthetic or public data**. Do not use employer, customer, account, or personally identifying data.

1. **Finance performance and forecast:** a synthetic transaction/ledger dataset; data dictionary; budget-versus-actual workbook; reconciliation and exception log; driver-based 12-month forecast with base/upside/downside cases; SQL checks; a concise dashboard or report; and a one-page memo that separates measured results from assumptions.
2. **Payments analysis:** synthetic transaction-level records plus an optional public Banco de México aggregate series for context; documented definitions for approval, refunds, disputes, and net contribution; SQL transformations; control checks; segment/cohort analysis with sample-size caveats; visual summary; and a short English presentation.

For either project, preserve the raw inputs, source/version/date, assumptions, formulas or queries, test cases, reconciliation evidence, known limitations, and instructions to reproduce one key result. The month-nine investment analysis can be a further learning artifact, not a substitute for the two core projects.

## Connect evidence to applications

- **Résumé:** describe a learning project as a project, not employment. Use action + method + verifiable output; include numbers only when they can be reproduced. Represent internship or other work separately and accurately.
- **Interviews:** rehearse a 30–45 minute Excel reconciliation/forecast exercise, a short SQL join/aggregation task, a finance case explaining profit versus cash, and a five-minute English project walkthrough with follow-up questions.
- **Manageable routine:** reserve one weekly session to search employer sites and record 3–5 relevant postings; compare actual required/preferred skills and eligibility, then submit up to 1–3 well-matched applications per week when suitable roles exist. This is a planning suggestion, not a market-volume expectation. A remote label alone does not prove Mexico eligibility.
- **Every two weeks:** review completed project checks, recurring errors, application responses, and interview feedback. Change the next two-week priority based on this evidence; do not add a certification just to fill time.

## First seven days

1. Confirm privately the available weekly hours, software/computer access, training budget, work authorization, graduation timing, English/Spanish working level, and acceptable work arrangement.
2. Complete the short Excel, SQL, finance, and communication baseline exercises on the [overview](./overview#start-with-a-practical-baseline).
3. Create a small synthetic ledger and write its data dictionary, source note, assumptions, and expected reconciliation totals.
4. Check the Citi and PepsiCo pages in the dated opportunity snapshot directly; record whether they are open and whether the detailed eligibility fits.
5. Explain one finding in English for two minutes and ask a reviewer to check the calculation and clarity.
